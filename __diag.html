How the model thinks

From past results to a fair price.

Four moves. No black box — each one is written up in full on the methodology page.

01 · INGEST
1,284

completed matches reconciled against a second source this season. Disagreements are flagged, never averaged.

02 · RATE
2 × 147

attack and defence ratings, fitted per competition with a ~180-day half-life so old form fades.

03 · SIMULATE
50,000

runs per fixture. Counting outcomes gives every result, scoreline and total-goals probability.

04 · SCORE
Brier

and log loss, published monthly against a naive baseline — including the months we lose.

01

Ingest and reconcile

Results, lineups and shot data arrive per competition and are checked against an independent second source. Where the two disagree, the fixture is pulled from training until a human resolves it. We would rather drop a match than learn from a wrong one.

02

Rate every team

Each side carries an attack and a defence rating relative to its competition average, fitted by maximum likelihood. Recent matches count for more via exponential decay, and home advantage is fitted per competition rather than assumed to be one global constant.

03

Simulate the fixture

Ratings become goal expectancies, which define a scoring model with a correlation term — because a side chasing the game concedes differently from one protecting a lead. The match is then played out 50,000 times.

04

Score ourselves honestly

Hit rate flatters a model that only ever backs favourites, so we use Brier score and log loss instead. Both are published monthly beside a naive baseline. A model that cannot beat the baseline is not adding value, and if ours fails to, the report says so.
